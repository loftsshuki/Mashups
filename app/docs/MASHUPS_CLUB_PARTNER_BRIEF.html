01

Release marketing has a signal problem

A label can commission posts and still finish a campaign without knowing which hook worked, which creator audience mattered, whether attribution consolidated, or what to repeat next.

Too much guesswork

Teams choose one clip, send broad briefs, and discover creative fit only after the release window is moving.

Too little control

Creator context, commercial use, platform availability, and claim handling are often split across email threads and vendors.

Weak evidence

Raw views and follower counts do not explain whether a post outperformed that creator's normal content or generated repeatable lift.

02

Mashups makes the release testable

We combine hook strategy, creator activation, rights operations, attribution, and performance reporting in one bounded campaign.

Before launch

Identify three high-potential 8-15 second moments and match each to native creator behaviors.

During activation

Recruit verified creators, enforce approved contexts, issue usage proof, and map every post to the official sound where possible.

After publication

Compare hooks, niches, and treatments against creator baselines and show the label where follow-on budget has the strongest evidence.

The label keeps control. Mashups supplies execution and evidence.

04

Control stays with the music team

The standard pilot is not an unrestricted remix grant, permanent catalog license, or automatic permission for advertising.

  • Approve or reject every hook
  • Choose permitted creator categories
  • Exclude sensitive content and contexts
  • Set platforms, territories, and term
  • Separate organic and sponsored use
  • Separate creator posts and paid media
  • Define editing and remix permissions
  • Nominate a claims and Content ID contact

Public activation requires confirmed authority for both the sound recording and underlying composition, plus any samples, interpolations, featured performers, and other approvals relevant to the campaign. Mashups does not treat delivery to a social platform as proof of commercial clearance.
