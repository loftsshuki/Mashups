Operating thesisDo not sell access to songs. Sell a controlled release campaign that turns one approved hook into attributable creator activity.

01

Decision

Enter electronic music as a focused beachhead under the working name Mashups Club. Lead with tech house, minimal/deep tech, accessible vocal house, and selected melodic house. Add high-energy techno as a specialized lane rather than the center of the offer.

Yes to electronic. House first. No to becoming another royalty-free EDM library.

Do not become

  • A generic stock-music catalog.
  • A streaming service competing on track count.
  • A tool that calls every electronic scene “EDM.”
  • A promotion vendor promising viral views.
  • A remix platform that treats uncleared samples as harmless.

Become

  • The launch system for independent electronic releases.
  • The place labels test three hooks before spending heavily.
  • The creator network that guarantees qualified activity, not virality.
  • The rights layer that keeps approved uses attributable and claim-ready.
  • The performance dataset connecting tracks, hooks, creators, and outcomes.

Mashups remains genre-expandable. Electronic music is the first concentrated market in which the track-to-hook-to-rights-to-lift loop can be proven quickly.

Opportunity

Independent labels are not short of music. They are short of reliable creator execution, clean permission workflows, and evidence connecting creator activity to release outcomes.

02Match the right creators
03Encode approved use

What labels struggle with

  • Choosing which 8-15 seconds should lead the campaign.
  • Recruiting creators who fit the record rather than accepting generic placements.
  • Keeping creator instructions, approvals, disclosures, and rights consistent.
  • Preventing automated claims and fragmented “original sound” attribution.
  • Separating real creative lift from follower count, paid traffic, or one lucky post.
  • Learning before release instead of explaining failure after the marketing budget is spent.

The concise explanation

30-second opportunity

Mashups turns one approved electronic release into a controlled creator campaign. We identify three high-potential hooks, match them to verified creators, issue track-specific usage proof, keep every post tied to the official sound where possible, and report which hook, creator niche, and treatment produced lift. The label keeps creative and rights control. Mashups supplies the execution and evidence.

The product is not access to a track. The product is a repeatable release experiment with rights and attribution built in.

06

Rights boundary

Electronic music can produce cleaner one-stop opportunities, but samples, loops, vocalists, producer agreements, publishing splits, and distributor controls make “the artist owns it” an unsafe assumption.

Minimum track acceptance

  • Master controller and composition controllers identified.
  • All writer and publisher shares documented to 100%.
  • Samples, interpolations, loops, and vocal sources disclosed and cleared.
  • Featured artists, producers, and remixers approved where required.
  • Organic, sponsored, paid-media, editing, looping, speed-change, and remix permissions separated.
  • Platform, territory, term, survival, withdrawal, and content-category rules encoded.
  • Content ID administrator and allowlisting or claim-release procedure confirmed.
  • Official sound identifiers and distributor delivery status collected.

The sound recording and the underlying musical composition are separate copyrighted works. Permission from only the master owner is not sufficient when the composition is controlled elsewhere. Electronic tracks with samples or interpolations can add further rights layers.

Platform operating requirement

Mashups needs a platform sound map for every accepted track: ISRC, TikTok sound ID, Instagram audio ID, YouTube recording asset, distributor, Content ID policy, supported territories, commercial-use status, and official-sound deep links.

A public license certificate proves the grant. It does not automatically stop a platform bot from muting the post.

Where possible, creators should build around an exact Mashups-approved hook and then attach the destination platform's official sound. That preserves usage aggregation and reduces the chance that the post becomes a disconnected “original sound.”

Legal boundary: This playbook is business strategy, not legal advice. Music counsel should approve supplier agreements, campaign grants, creator terms, promotional rules, and material territory-specific deviations.

  • Unknown music with no hook utility chosen solely because it is easy to license.

Research each account

  1. Find a release from the last 90 days or an announced upcoming release.
  2. Identify the exact 8-15 second moment that could support a creator concept.
  3. Map the sound to two creator niches and one visual treatment.
  4. Identify likely master, publishing, distributor, and management contacts.
  5. Check whether the official sound is delivered across target platforms.
  6. Record one useful observation in the CRM before sending anything.

Message architecture

Lead with the label's release problem, not the Mashups feature list.

AudienceTheir concernMashups explanation
Label owner“Will this create meaningful activity without damaging the record?”Controlled creators, approved hooks, explicit use boundaries, attributable reporting.
Marketing lead“How is this different from seeding?”Hook testing, creator matching, normalized baselines, post-level evidence, repeatable learning.
Artist or producer“Will creators make the track feel cheap or off-brand?”Artist-approved concepts, creator categories, sensitive-use exclusions, and optional review.
Rights or legal“What exactly are we granting?”Track-, platform-, territory-, term-, and use-specific policy with immutable license snapshots.
Distributor“Will this create claims and support work?”Official sound mapping, channel registration, claim canary, evidence packages, SLA reporting.
Manager“Does this help the next release?”Private Hook Lab plus a campaign that shows which audiences and creative treatments to scale.

Core claims

  • Controlled: the partner approves the track, hooks, creator categories, use types, platforms, territories, and term.
  • Measurable: Mashups evaluates creator posts against their own recent baselines instead of comparing raw follower counts.
  • Operationally safe: every campaign has a rights record, official-sound map, claims contact, and escalation path.
  • Incremental: the partner learns which hook and creator treatment deserves further budget.
  • Bounded: one release can be tested without granting permanent catalog-wide rights.

Discovery questions

  1. How do you currently select the clip that leads a release?
  2. What creator activity has worked, failed, or been impossible to explain?
  3. Do you buy posts, seed organically, work through agencies, or combine approaches?
  4. Which creator categories would make the artist feel credible rather than overexposed?
  5. Which contexts must be excluded?
  6. Who controls the master and composition, and are there samples or interpolations?
  7. Who administers Content ID and platform delivery?
  8. What result would justify a second paid campaign?
  9. Which release four to eight weeks out is the cleanest test?

Required next step

Do not end with “we'll stay in touch.” Book the rights review, receive the track packet, or agree on the date the next release becomes available for evaluation.

Qualification

Prioritize partners where clean execution and a convincing result are both plausible. Easy rights with unusable music are not enough; popular music with impossible rights is not enough.

Rights readiness25 points Release timing and urgency20 points Current social signal20 points Hook and creator utility15 points Partner responsiveness10 points Data and case-study access10 points
Partner priority0 / 100 - Red

Decision thresholds

  • 75-100 / Green: move to rights review and pilot design immediately.
  • 55-74 / Yellow: continue discovery but resolve the lowest scoring area before committing creator spend.
  • 0-54 / Red: nurture or decline; do not force a pilot for logo value.

Hard disqualifiers

  • Known uncleared sample or unresolved ownership dispute.
  • No authority to approve the intended platforms and use categories.
  • Refusal to define a Content ID contact or claim response process.
  • Demand for guaranteed views, streams, chart position, or artificial engagement.
  • Release timing that makes proper rights and creator preparation impossible.

15

90-day pilot

The goal is not a large catalog. The goal is evidence that clean electronic rights, creator execution, and paid partner demand can form a repeatable loop.

Days 1-15: package the offer

  • Finalize the one-track pilot grant and rights intake questionnaire with counsel.
  • Build the account list, scorecard, CRM, outreach sequence, and partner brief.
  • Recruit 25 initial creators across fashion, fitness, nightlife, automotive, travel, gaming, and dance.
  • Prepare a sample three-hook treatment and example campaign report using non-confidential material.
  • Complete 10 discovery conversations before locking pricing.

Days 16-30: sign founding partners

  • Contact 25 Tier A and B accounts with track-specific messages.
  • Secure five signed pilot partners and at least 15 nominated tracks.
  • Clear five campaign tracks and reject anything that fails chain-of-title review.
  • Map official platform sounds and test Content ID handling with canary posts.
  • Approve three hooks and five creative treatments for each campaign track.

Days 31-60: run campaigns

  • Activate 10-25 creators per track in controlled weekly waves.
  • Monitor publication compliance, claims, deletion, attribution, and early performance.
  • Pay base rewards for qualified posts and bonuses only on verified outcomes.
  • Hold seven-day label reviews and adapt creator matching without changing rights scope silently.
  • Capture creator and partner interviews for case-study evidence.

Days 61-90: convert evidence

  • Deliver 30-day reports with hook, niche, treatment, claims, and normalized lift analysis.
  • Ask every partner for a paid second campaign, next-release Hook Lab, testimonial, and one referral.
  • Publish only evidence the partner explicitly approves.
  • Approach distributors and label-services companies with cohort-level results.
  • Decide whether to deepen house, expand techno, or test UK garage/drum and bass based on actual creator performance.
