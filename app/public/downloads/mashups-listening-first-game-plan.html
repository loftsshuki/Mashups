01 // Executive decision

Invert the company.

Do not throw away the studio. Move it behind the audience experience. The feed becomes the front door, great mashups become the product, and creation becomes the high-value behavior of a committed minority.

Proceed with a listener-first, artist-sanctioned 90-day experiment.

Prove that people repeatedly consume excellent mashups, that a small creator cohort can supply them, and that artists or release-budget holders will fund campaigns. Do not pursue major-label catalog, a broad open network, or a large financing plan before those three facts exist.

Operating posture
GO

Conditional. Narrow. Measured. Every feature must answer a live business question.

02 // The inversion

From tool to destination.

The research exposed the dangerous assumption: liking mashups does not mean wanting to make them. The corrected thesis lets the audience be large even if creators stay rare.

Retire this thesis
Everyone arrives ready to make a mashup.

This forces every visitor through a high-effort creation flow before they understand the value. It also makes an empty or unfamiliar catalog fatal.

Run this thesis
Everyone arrives ready to hear something impossible.

Instant entertainment demonstrates the magic. Listening, voting, and requesting are low-friction. Creation appears when curiosity becomes intent.

03 // Network roles

Four jobs. One system.

Do not judge every person by whether they publish. Listening, selecting, making, and activating each create a different form of value.

01

Listeners

Swipe, replay, vote, save, follow, and share. They are the audience and the largest potential cohort.

Value created: attention, taste signals, distribution
02

Selectors

Request pairings, curate crates, identify rising combinations, and vote before anything is produced.

Value created: ideas, demand telemetry, curation
03

Makers

Use three machine-generated starts, improve the winner, claim fan requests, and enter artist challenges.

Value created: content supply, culture, creative status
04

Artists

Opt in music, set controls, launch challenges, promote winning versions, and learn what their fans respond to.

Value created: permission, audience, recognition, budget

05 // Content machine

Never publish the bad take.

A listening product lives or dies on hit rate. Automation produces candidates. Human taste decides what earns distribution.

Supply 01

Owned green catalog

Use Mashups-owned material to test native video, exports, listening behavior, and unrestricted mechanics without rights ambiguity.

Supply 02

Invited makers

Five to ten strong creators receive compatible sources, requests, and clear weekly briefs.

Supply 03

Artist drops

Each signed artist creates a concentrated event with recognition and a built-in audience.

Signal 04

The request market

Fans propose Track A x Track B. Votes create a production queue and tell partnerships which unavailable music has real demand.

Program 05

Editorial cadence

Mashup of the Day. Friday Drop Battle. Artist Week. Scene-versus-scene. Staff Pick. The feed is programmed like a station, not filled like a database.

Reward 06

Status first

Features, badges, fixed prizes, commissions, and artist recognition. Do not create a royalty bureaucracy for pennies.

Non-negotiable quality gate

One proudly shareable result among three.

Every compatible pair must produce three genuinely different arrangements. At least one should be rated 4 out of 5 by a majority of target listeners. Bad vocal collisions, obvious key clashes, smeared timing, and broken phrase boundaries stay out of the feed.

06 // Rights operating model

Permission becomes product behavior.

Do not treat a track as simply licensed or unlicensed. Every capability reads the track's Rights Passport.

01

Verify

Master control, publishing control, splits, samples, performers, territories.

02

Configure

Pairing scope, transforms, explicit-content rules, fork status, withdrawals.

03

Create

In-app combinations only when both passports authorize the behavior.

04

Publish

Attribution, source lineage, territories, takedown status, audit trail.

05

Expand

Video, platform sharing, advertising, or distribution only through added grants.

Enabled in the first pilot

  • Artist-direct and Mashups-owned tracks
  • In-app playback and public Mashups links
  • Three controlled arrangement options
  • Attribution and internal lineage
  • Artist pairing controls and withdrawal
  • Owned-music video export experiments

Disabled until explicitly granted

  • Standalone audio downloads for licensed tracks
  • Licensed social video exports
  • Paid advertising or brand synchronization
  • DSP distribution
  • Cash creator royalty promises
  • Licensed remix-of-remix forking
07 // Business model

Campaign money first. Consumer money later.

The first payer is the release-budget holder: label, manager, distributor, sponsor, or established self-releasing artist. Consumer subscriptions are measured later, not assumed.

10 // Day-90 decision

Do not blend the outcomes.

The pilot can support three different companies. Choose the one the evidence earned.

Outcome A // Network

Listeners return and artists renew.

  • Quality gate passes
  • Day-7 return reaches target
  • Shares and requests compound
  • Two of three partners renew
GO: fund the listening-first social network and expand artist-direct catalog.
Outcome B // Campaign platform

Artists care more than consumers.

  • Campaigns produce useful content
  • Partners show budget
  • General listener return is weak
  • Operations remain manageable
PIVOT: sell sanctioned remix campaigns and keep the network as the delivery layer.
Outcome C // Stop

Neither side pulls.

  • Quality misses after two iterations
  • Listeners do not return
  • Artists will not promote or renew
  • No buyer demonstrates willingness to pay
STOP: preserve useful audio and rights components, but end the platform thesis.
11 // Build discipline

Build the proof. Pause the empire.

The repository contains far more surface area than the experiment needs. Reorder and narrow before adding.

Build now

  1. Listening-first mobile feed
  2. Reliable short-form playback and replay
  3. Audio quality benchmark and editorial approval queue
  4. Listener event instrumentation
  5. Request, vote, and creator-claim loop
  6. Artist campaign pages and reporting
  7. Minimal Rights Passport and behavior gates
  8. Owned-music native video share experiment

Pause now

  1. Major-label negotiations
  2. Standalone licensed audio export
  3. Cash creator royalty cascades
  4. Blockchain or token accounting
  5. Graph database migration
  6. Native mobile applications
  7. Open user uploads of commercial music
  8. Broad marketplace, teams, and agency complexity
12 // Risk register

Know what can kill it.

The largest risk is no longer simply copyright. It is building expensive rights infrastructure around a behavior that never becomes habitual.

Critical

Great mashups remain too rare

The engine generates technically valid but culturally weak outputs.

Control: compatible-pair gating, real stems, human curation, two-iteration kill rule.
Critical

Listening is novelty, not habit

People enjoy one collision and never return.

Control: programmed cadence, artist events, requests, follows, and Day-7 decision gate.
High

Unknown music removes the hook

Owned demos test technology but fail to represent artist fandom.

Control: run artist-direct recognition test before judging the final thesis.
High

Links do not travel

Walled-garden sharing produces weak referral behavior.

Control: test native video with owned music immediately; negotiate licensed clips only after evidence.
High

Artists like it but will not promote

Supply without audience distribution creates a cold-start catalog.

Control: promotion commitment is part of pilot participation and measured per campaign.
High

Artists like it but nobody pays

Positive interviews conceal the absence of a budget holder.

Control: require one paid renewal, paid pilot, or credible signed LOI by day 90.
High

Rights work swallows the margin

Every track becomes a custom legal project.

Control: one-stop tracks, standard pilot terms, manual time tracking, and strict exceptions.
Medium

Hook or Spotify closes the gap

A larger platform adds better cross-song creation and distribution.

Control: compete on quality, scene depth, campaigns, requests, and rights trust, not catalog size.

The North Star
Can a small group of creators make mashups that a much larger audience repeatedly wants to hear?

If yes, build the network. If artists value campaigns more than listeners value the destination, build the campaign platform. If neither side pulls, stop cleanly and preserve the useful technology.
